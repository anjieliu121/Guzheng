Overview:

This draft should include moderately developed content for each and every section of your thesis following an organizational principle that is appropriate to your discipline, your project, and your non-specialist readership. Your reader should be able to see the full arc of how your argument and your reasoning develop. No more brain dumps here!

Expectations and Criteria for Evaluation:

By this point, you should have at least 25 pages of body text (starting with the first page of the Introduction and ending with the last page of the Conclusion, and formatted as described below), plus updated front matter, references, short biography, and optional graphics and/or appendices, using the thesis template. Your argument should unfold clearly and logically, with sections building upon one another sequentially. All your primary evidence and analysis should be included. Note that you many need to submit a rough draft that is longer than 25 pages in order to accomplish this!

Before submitting, read through your draft in full and make sure to respond to feedback you have received thus far.  

In order to prepare you for the kind of scrutiny the final draft of your thesis is likely to receive, this draft will be graded on quality as well as completion. While we do not expect a polished final draft, we expect you to have put some effort into addressing feedback you have received and cleaning up the messiest aspects of your writing and organization. Please see the rubric attached to this assignment for more information about criteria we will use to evaluate your thesis.

Your full draft should give your reader the impression of a cohesive document. It must include the following elements in order to be considered complete:

A title page (includes your name, working thesis title, and supervisor's and second reader's name and department) 
An updated table of contents
An acknowledgements page (after the signature page, before the abstract or body text)
An updated, one-paragraph abstract with key terms (max. 300 words for abstract)
An introduction, at least mostly revised
Section headings and subheadings as applicable
A clearly defined argument (your primary claim) supported by logical reasoning and appropriate scholarly evidence
Page numbers, with the first page of your introduction as page 1*
In-text citations and attributions that adhere to APA citation style
Drafts of any graphics, illustrations, images (i.e., figures), or tables you plan to include
Figure labels, captions, legends, and notes, if applicable, formatted according to APA guidelines
An initial draft of your conclusion
A References section, complete and formatted according to APA style
Appendices, if applicable (most of you don't need appendices and may delete this section)
A one- or two-paragraph author biography as the final page of the thesis

Formatting:

Use the Thesis Template for your submitted drafts, including this one.

All drafts of your thesis that you submit for NSC 371 should be double-spaced, with one-inch margins, using a standard 12-point serif font such as Times New Roman (preferred). Make sure to include your name, working thesis title, and page numbering on all drafts; this should be no problem if you are using the template.
